# Ad Effectiveness

Use this skill when evaluating ad creative, briefing agencies or freelancers, running pre-spend creative assessment, or diagnosing why advertising is not generating returns.

---

## The Fundamental Tension

All advertising serves one of two goals. Establish which one you are making before writing a single word of brief.

**Brand advertising** (long-term demand generation):
- Builds future demand by creating positive memory structures in people who are not currently buying
- Emotional, narrative-driven, no hard CTA
- Effects delayed 3–18 months; ROI measured in years
- Harder to justify in quarterly reviews; higher long-term profit impact

**Direct response** (short-term demand capture):
- Captures present demand from people already in the market
- Rational, targeted, clear CTA, urgency-driven
- Effects visible immediately in click-through, conversion, ROAS
- Easy to justify; lower long-term brand equity contribution

Most SaaS companies produce only direct response. This is rational in the short term and damaging in the long term. Binet & Field's IPA analysis of 700+ campaigns found that emotional campaigns generate **2x the profit impact** of rational campaigns over a 3-year horizon. The reason: rational content is forgotten once the offer ends. Emotional content builds memory structures that persist.

Both are necessary. Use direct response to capture demand now. Use brand advertising to build the pool of future buyers who will choose you when they are ready.

## System1's 3F Framework

System1 Group tests ads with real consumers and measures emotional response in real time. Apply their framework — Fame, Feeling, Fluency — to evaluate any piece of creative before approving it for spend.

### Fame

Does the ad grab attention and generate feeling?

Fame is the scale of emotional response — how strongly the ad makes people feel anything at all. An ad that generates no emotional response is forgettable. It leaves no memory trace. Media spend behind a flat ad is effectively wasted.

- **High fame**: viewer stops scrolling, leans in, remembers it a week later, shares it
- **Low fame**: ad plays, viewer looks away, no trace remains

Fame is the prerequisite. Without it, Feeling and Fluency do not matter.

### Feeling

What specific emotion does the ad trigger?

Not all emotion is equal. Positive emotions build long-term brand associations. Negative emotions can drive short-term action but erode brand equity over time.

- **Builds long-term brand equity**: joy, warmth, amusement, inspiration, nostalgia, surprise
- **Avoid for brand advertising**: fear, contempt, disgust, anxiety
- **Fear and urgency**: works short-term (security software, limited-time offers), damages brand when overused — trains the audience to associate your brand with threat

Identify the primary emotion before evaluating any creative. If you cannot name a specific emotion after one viewing, the ad is flat.

### Fluency

Does the brand land? Can a viewer identify who made this ad?

A highly emotional ad that fails to link to the brand is wasted spend. You have entertained someone on behalf of a competitor. Brand fluency requires:

- Brand name or logo appearing at natural story moments, not only in the final frame
- Distinctive brand assets (color, character, sound, tone) embedded in the creative
- Consistent tone across executions so the brand becomes recognizable before the logo appears

**The fluency test**: mentally remove the logo. Would the target audience still know who made it? If not, fluency is weak.

## System1's 1-5 Star Rating

System1 scores every tested ad on a 1–5 star scale based on the emotional response of real viewers. Use this scale to calibrate creative before committing media budget.

| Stars | Meaning | What it looks like |
|-------|---------|-------------------|
| 1 | Flat. No emotional response. | Generic product demo, stock footage, feature list read aloud |
| 2 | Weak signal. Mild reaction. | Some positive feeling, quickly forgotten, no brand association |
| 3 | Good. Positive feeling, some brand linkage. | Viewers feel something; brand lands |
| 4 | Excellent. Strong emotion, clear brand linkage. | Memorable, shareable, builds lasting associations |
| 5 | Exceptional. Fluent Device territory. | Viewer has an ongoing relationship with the creative world |

Only approximately 1% of ads tested by System1 reach 5 stars. Aim for 3+ stars for brand activity. Direct response creative can succeed at 2 stars if targeting and offer are strong.

A 1-star brand ad is not just ineffective — it actively wastes budget and trains the media plan to reward bad creative.

## Why Emotional Ads Outperform Rational

The brain processes emotional and rational content differently.

Rational content — features, price comparisons, specifications — is processed by working memory. It is conscious, deliberate, and temporary. Once the offer ends or the viewer moves on, the rational case dissolves.

Emotional content creates **memory structures**: networks of associations, feelings, and images linked to a brand. These structures persist without reinforcement. They activate at the point of purchase — often 12 months after the viewer last saw the ad.

Key findings from Binet & Field's IPA Databank (the largest advertising effectiveness database in the world):
- Emotional campaigns generate **2x the profit impact** of rational campaigns over 3+ years
- **31%** of award-winning emotional campaigns generated very large business effects, versus **16%** for rational campaigns
- The longer the campaign runs, the larger the emotional advantage compounds

Emotional brand advertising is not a luxury or a branding indulgence. It is the highest long-term ROI activity in the marketing budget.

## The Fluent Device

A Fluent Device is a recurring creative element — a character, scenario, world, or tonal signature — that appears across multiple ads over time.

The mechanism: each new execution builds on the viewer's existing emotional relationship with the device. Rather than starting from zero, the ad inherits the warmth, familiarity, and associations built by every prior execution. This is compound interest for advertising.

**What qualifies as a Fluent Device:**
- A character with a consistent personality embedded in a recognizable creative world
- A recurring scenario or format that the viewer recognizes and anticipates
- A distinctive tonal signature that makes the brand identifiable before the logo appears

**Examples:**
- Compare the Market (UK): Meerkat characters. Each execution builds on viewer affection for Aleksandr. Series has run 15+ years and is the brand's primary growth driver.
- Cadbury Gorilla (2007): Extended the tonal world of unexpected joy across multiple executions. The gorilla is not the Fluent Device — the emotional register is.
- Old Spice: The absurdist confidence tone becomes the device itself. No single character required — the world is the device.

**Why most brands do not have a Fluent Device**: they reset creative every 6–12 months in search of novelty. Each reset destroys the accumulated emotional relationship. A consistently executed 3-star Fluent Device outperforms a one-off 4-star ad over a three-year period.

Build a world, not just an ad.

## Storytelling Structure for Brand Ads

The most effective emotional ads follow a consistent narrative arc. Apply this when evaluating or briefing story-based creative.

1. **Setup** — a relatable situation the viewer recognizes from their own life. Not aspirational. Grounded and true.
2. **Tension** — something is wrong, missing, difficult, or at stake. Creates emotional investment before the brand appears.
3. **Resolution** — the situation resolves. The viewer feels relief, joy, warmth, or inspiration.
4. **Brand reveal** — the brand emerges as the context that enabled the resolution. Not an interruption. A natural conclusion.

The brand should feel like the entity that makes resolution possible — not a logo appended to someone else's story.

**Common structural failures:**
- Product appearing before emotional investment is established
- Resolution happening without brand involvement
- Ending on a CTA in a brand film (the feeling should be the ending, not an instruction)
- Starting with the problem in a way that makes the viewer feel bad before they have a reason to stay

## Briefing for Effectiveness

A brief that produces effective creative has exactly one objective, one insight, and one emotional territory. Every additional objective dilutes creative focus.

**The 9 components of a good brief:**

1. **Background/context** — the business problem the creative must solve
2. **Target audience** — a specific segment with a real human insight, not a demographic range ("marketing managers at mid-market SaaS who are privately worried their campaigns look the same as last year's")
3. **Single human truth** — the one insight that makes the creative feel real and earned, before the brand enters
4. **Emotional territory** — primary emotion plus secondary emotion. Not "be funny." "Warmth with moments of wry, self-aware humor."
5. **Role of the brand** — how does the brand fit into the human truth? What does it make possible that was not possible before?
6. **Distinctive asset requirements** — which brand assets must appear, and how naturally they should integrate
7. **Mandatories and non-negotiables** — legal, regulatory, brand guardrails
8. **Executional guidance** — what to avoid, what tone is off-limits, what has failed before
9. **Success criteria** — how the output will be evaluated before media spend is committed

## How to Evaluate Creative

Apply these five questions to any piece of creative before approving it for production or spend.

1. **Does it make you feel something?** Watch it. Name the emotion. If you cannot name a specific emotion after one viewing, it is flat. Do not approve flat creative for brand campaigns.

2. **Is the feeling positive or negative?** Identify the primary emotion. If it is negative — fear, urgency, anxiety — ask whether this creative is designed for short-term conversion or brand building. If brand building, reject and rebrief.

3. **Would you know it was ours without the logo?** Remove the logo mentally. Does the tone, color, character, or world signal your brand? If not, you do not yet have a distinctive brand identity in this creative.

4. **Does it use our distinctive assets?** List your DBAs (distinctive brand assets): color palette, typography, sound, character, tagline, shape. Confirm they appear naturally in the creative, not bolted on in the final frame.

5. **Does it fit into a long-term creative world?** Could this execution be part of a series? If the answer is no, decide consciously whether you are making a one-off or beginning a Fluent Device. Both are valid — but only one builds compound returns.

## Quick Diagnostics

**"Why is our advertising not working?"**
Work through this in order:
1. Are you running brand advertising at all, or only direct response?
2. Is your creative generating emotional response? (Fame)
3. Is the emotion positive? (Feeling)
4. Is the brand landing clearly? (Fluency)
5. Are you running creative long enough for brand effects to compound?
6. Are you resetting creative too frequently before a Fluent Device can develop?

**"Should we use long-form or short-form?"**
- Long-form (60s+): brand advertising, emotional storytelling, cold audiences who do not know you yet
- Short-form (6–15s): retargeting, audiences who already know you, reinforcing existing associations
- Do not use short-form alone for cold brand building — there is not enough time to establish Fame, Feeling, and Fluency simultaneously

**"How do we test this before committing budget?"**
- System1 offers quantitative pre-testing (Star Rating plus emotion trace) for any format
- Internal pre-test: show the ad to five people outside your company. Ask what they felt and who made it. Do not explain the brief first.
- If three of five cannot name the emotion or the brand: do not approve for brand spend

## Producing AI-Render Ad Briefs (Higgsfield / Veo / Runway / Sora)

When turning a concept into a video brief for an autonomous AI render platform, produce ONE
self-contained document per video so the operator can paste blocks in order without
cross-referencing files.

**Structure each brief as:**
1. A "Block 0 — Project brief" paste that establishes the world, characters (precise physical
   descriptions for cross-generation consistency), visual style, tone, camera language, and
   what to avoid.
2. One paste-ready fenced block per shot, in order. Each shot block must be fully
   self-sufficient — re-state character and set descriptions verbatim in every block, since
   generations may not share context.
3. Embedded brand-code table (colours, morph/motif behaviour, sonic logo, tagline, end card,
   tone guardrails) — identical across every video's brief so the Fluent Device cannot drift.
4. Named primary emotion per beat, and ≥5 brand-code placements mapped to shots.
5. VO script, music/sound direction, assembly notes, cutdown briefs (per aspect ratio and
   length), and the render scorecard.

**Anti-screenshot rule:** never brief a browser, website, screen, UI, or dashboard into a
story shot — it is the single strongest left-brain penalty. Replace any product-UI reveal
with an in-world reveal that keeps the reframing surprise via metaphor.

**Score every returned render** against `references/render-scorecard.md` before accepting it:
≥4 right-brain points, zero left-brain flags, and pass the 3F gate.
